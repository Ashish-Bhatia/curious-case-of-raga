## Direct official record: what is established

- Gandhi's longer recorded speech discusses Hindu, Islamic, Sikh and other religious symbolism, fearlessness and non-violence before the disputed exchange (printed pp. 130–133).
- The official transcript records Modi objecting to alleged attribution of violence to the whole Hindu community on printed p. 133; several of Gandhi's adjacent words are explicitly expunged.
- Other members repeated competing interpretations; the Speaker asked speakers not to hurt religious sentiments. These are **records of reactions and interventions**, not adjudications that either disputed interpretation is factually established.
- The later printed parliamentary transcript has edited/expunged passages. It cannot independently resolve exactly what the removed words were, punctuation or the full audiovisual referent. A news quote alone cannot bypass this evidentiary gap.
- Distinguish the **date spoken (1 July)**, the **Sansad TV video-page publication (4 July)**, and the **edited Parliamentary transcription and removal of words**.

## Key verification question

Did Gandhi's relevant full Hindi statement, including gesture, earlier and later qualifying sentences and interruption exchanges, refer to **all Hindus** or to **BJP/RSS politicians who present themselves as Hindu**? A fair answer requires the *unedited* recording with timecodes and independently checked translation. Even once transcribed, the meaning of a rhetorical rebuke may remain contestable and is not automatically a checkable proposition of fact.
