# RAGA-2019-0001 — Supreme Court attribution after Rafale document ruling

## Attribution and context
The 14 November 2019 Supreme Court judgment, paragraphs 26–27, describes Gandhi's statements to several media persons on 10 April 2019 as attributing to the Court an assertion that Prime Minister Narendra Modi was a thief and had stolen Air Force funds for Anil Ambani. This is the Court's description, **not a certified verbatim transcript** of every word spoken.

Contemporaneous [Times Now video page](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/videos/news/sc-agrees-that-chowkidar-chor-hai-rahul-gandhi/videoshow/68813481.cms) carries his remarks. The complete recording and preceding/following exchange have not been reviewed in this checkpoint.

## Testable proposition
Did the Supreme Court in its 10 April 2019 Rafale document-admissibility ruling find or endorse that Modi stole money or was corrupt in the Rafale procurement?

## Evidence ledger
1. **Judicial record, primary text reproduced by independent host.** [15 April 2019 Supreme Court Record of Proceedings](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/137054189/), Contempt Petition (Criminal) No. 3/2019. The bench said the 10 April decision addressed the admissibility of documents, and expressly disavowed the alleged finding attributed to it. Original signed judiciary-hosted version still missing.
2. **Judicial record, primary text reproduced by independent host.** [14 November 2019 judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/26499003/), *Yashwant Sinha v CBI* and connected matters, per S.K. Kaul J., judgment pp. 12–15, paras 26–33. It records the attribution, Gandhi's 22 April explanation, subsequent unconditional apology filed on 8 May, the Court's criticism, and closure of contempt proceedings with caution.
3. **Contemporaneous filmed claim lead.** [Times Now video, 10 April 2019](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/videos/news/sc-agrees-that-chowkidar-chor-hai-rahul-gandhi/videoshow/68813481.cms). Original wording cannot be certified from the video page's news summary alone.
4. **Independent secondary confirmation.** [Indian Express, 14 November 2019](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/rafale-case-sc-closes-defamation-case-against-rahul-gandhi-says-he-needs-to-be-more-careful/). This is corroboration, not substitute for judgment.

## Assessment
The Court's expressly limited admissibility decision did not find Modi guilty of theft, corruption or other offences. The attribution to the Court was therefore wrong. This supports **False** for the judicial-attribution proposition.

It does **not** establish that Gandhi deliberately misled the public. His affidavit asserted a good-faith, unintentional error in political campaigning, while the Court said the attribution should not have been made without checking. The later apology, though relevant, does not independently decide intent.

Neither this dossier nor the 2019 contempt judgment establishes a comprehensive factual verdict on the separate Rafale contract allegations. Those questions involve procurement records, pricing, offsets, the scope of judicial review and further evidence.

## Adversarial check
- Strongest context sympathetic to Gandhi: the 10 April decision **did** reject the government's legal objection to considering certain records in review; he described his remarks as a rhetorical flourish in the heat of campaigning.
- Strongest counterevidence: the Court's own 15 April clarification and 14 November disposition directly reject the attributed finding.
- Institutional caution: judicial review was limited in scope. Dismissing review petitions should not be overstated as a forensic audit of all procurement facts.

## Timeline

- 15 April 2019: Supreme Court clarifies it made no such finding and seeks explanation.
