## Attribution and context
The 14 November 2019 Supreme Court judgment, paragraphs 26–27, describes Gandhi's statements to several media persons on 10 April 2019 as attributing to the Court an assertion that Prime Minister Narendra Modi was a thief and had stolen Air Force funds for Anil Ambani. This is the Court's description, **not a certified verbatim transcript** of every word spoken.

Contemporaneous [Times Now video page](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/videos/news/sc-agrees-that-chowkidar-chor-hai-rahul-gandhi/videoshow/68813481.cms) carries his remarks. The complete recording and preceding/following exchange have not been reviewed in this checkpoint.

## Testable proposition
Did the Supreme Court in its 10 April 2019 Rafale document-admissibility ruling find or endorse that Modi stole money or was corrupt in the Rafale procurement?

## Assessment
The Court's expressly limited admissibility decision did not find Modi guilty of theft, corruption or other offences. The attribution to the Court was therefore wrong. This supports **False** for the judicial-attribution proposition.

It does **not** establish that Gandhi deliberately misled the public. His affidavit asserted a good-faith, unintentional error in political campaigning, while the Court said the attribution should not have been made without checking. The later apology, though relevant, does not independently decide intent.

Neither this dossier nor the 2019 contempt judgment establishes a comprehensive factual verdict on the separate Rafale contract allegations. Those questions involve procurement records, pricing, offsets, the scope of judicial review and further evidence.

## Adversarial check
- Strongest context sympathetic to Gandhi: the 10 April decision **did** reject the government's legal objection to considering certain records in review; he described his remarks as a rhetorical flourish in the heat of campaigning.
- Strongest counterevidence: the Court's own 15 April clarification and 14 November disposition directly reject the attributed finding.
- Institutional caution: judicial review was limited in scope. Dismissing review petitions should not be overstated as a forensic audit of all procurement facts.

## Checkpoint RAGA-20261015-003 — recovered 10 April judgment text (15 October 2026)

**Newly inspected judicial text:** [Supreme Court judgment dated 10 April 2019, *Yashwant Sinha & Ors. v Central Bureau of Investigation*, Review Petition (Criminal) 46/2019, 56-page PDF archived by Supreme Court Observer](https://www.scobserver.in/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/19__Judgement_10-Apr-2019.pdf). Pages 1 and 18 (Gogoi CJI / Kaul J majority opinion), and the operative paragraph 12 on printed page 18, were read and visually checked. The archive also carries Joseph J's concurring opinion on printed pages 1–38 (PDF pages 19–56). **Document status:** court-authored original judgment *reproduced on a third-party host*; neither a direct official Supreme Court-hosted copy nor its digital signature was authenticated in this checkpoint.

**Operative outcome (majority, para 12, printed p. 18):** the Court dismissed the Union of India's preliminary objections and ruled that the review petitions would be adjudicated on their merits while considering the disputed three documents. This was an adverse ruling for the government's attempt to exclude the documents, **not** a determination that Modi had stolen money or committed corruption. Justice Joseph concurred (printed p. 38, PDF p. 56).

**Contemporaneous executive record:** [Ministry of Defence, Press Information Bureau release, 10 April 2019 at 3:52 PM](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1570349&lang=2&reg=48) acknowledges that the Supreme Court allowed the documents to be considered and that the review petitions were still pending. This is primary evidence of the government's public position, **not** independent judicial authentication or proof that the rest of the ministry's defence was correct.

**Additional first-hand media lead:** [The Economic Times verified 10 April 2019 video upload, “SC agrees 'Chowkidar chor hai', says Rahul Gandhi on Rafale review case”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ck4uVc3MTjw) is a contemporaneous publisher recording link. Its description reports Gandhi alleging the Court accepted corruption in Rafale. **The full recording and exact uninterrupted Hindi/English wording remain unplayed and untranscribed**; do not silently convert publisher description to verbatim quotation.

**Adversarial correction:** The 10 April ruling genuinely admitted documents over the government's objection. Therefore any shorthand such as “the Court ruled for the government” would be false. The provisional **False** label still applies *only* to the specific attributed court finding of theft. The Court's assessment of the wider procurement and any allegation of knowing deception are outside this proposition.

**Blockers remaining:** (1) entire 10 April Rahul Gandhi recording with before/after context and original-language transcription; (2) judiciary-hosted 10 April original PDF **now retrieved and checked**; judiciary-hosted 14 November original PDF still not retrieved directly; (3) formal human editorial review. The primary 15 April clarification remains authenticated on the judiciary host. **Case remains review pending / unpublished. No editorial or publication flags changed.**
