## Exact question and why scope matters

The opposition and Agriculture Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan disputed whether the government already *gave MSP*. In the Lok Sabha record, Gandhi explicitly acknowledged MSP existed and **clarified he was demanding MSP with a legal guarantee**. Therefore, the shorthand interpretation that he asserted the government did not announce or procure any crops at MSP is contradicted by his own recorded clarification.

A legal entitlement might require a purchaser to pay at or above a specified floor or create an enforceable compensation mechanism. Announcing prices through Cabinet policy, paying a price for eligible procurement, and legally guaranteeing a price for every eligible farmer/transaction are legally distinct. Which model ought to be adopted, whether universal procurement is feasible and whether the government is politically 'ready' are policy evaluations beyond this narrow factual verdict.

The printed record for the surrounding speech contains explicit expungements elsewhere, particularly the earlier religion exchange. The MSP clarification on printed p.148 is visible and **not itself marked expunged**. Do not import words deleted in other passages.

## Disaggregated propositions and checks

1. **Was MSP offered in 2024? — Yes.** The 19 June pre-claim Cabinet decision announces prices for Kharif 2024-25; government's 2023 parliamentary reply documents procurement programs. Gandhi acknowledged this explicitly. **Supported**.
2. **Were announced/procured MSPs equivalent to a universal binding statutory price guarantee on 1 July 2024? — No evidence of such a general law in the records inspected.** Contemporaneous ministry materials describe administrative price-fixing and conditional procurement, with a committee on MSP reform. Subsequent government written answer still addresses the sought guarantee as a separate question. **Supported with a statutory-scope caveat.** To make an exhaustive legal claim, verify the India Code statute register and any state-specific commodity statutes.
3. **Did the government refuse categorically to ever legislate? — Not established.** Gandhi's statement about readiness was political interpretation. The 26 July ministerial reply declined to make a definite commitment while referring to the committee. An absence of commitment is not proof of permanent refusal. **Not part of the scored factual verdict**.
4. **Does ‘legal guarantee’ mean the government must buy every crop? — Not necessarily.** A right may be enforceable via market regulation, deficiency payment, compensation or procurement. Do not assume a single policy design.
5. **Did the Agriculture Minister claim government already buys at MSP? — Yes.** That specific defence is also supported by documentary policy and procurement records. Gandhi's clarification, not the government's existence-of-MSP claim, is the issue under review.

## Strongest contrary evidence / falsification attempt

- The government's **19 June 2024** MSP decision and detailed historical procurement totals directly rebut any version of this claim read as ‘the government gives no MSP’. That interpretation would be **False**, but is *not* the version Gandhi clarified in the transcript.
- Government's 2023 reply describes effective price support for eligible paddy, wheat, oilseeds, pulses and other products, so ‘farmers get no legal protection or support whatsoever’ would also be an overstatement.
- Sugarcane has a distinct statutory/control-order fair-and-remunerative-price framework. It would be wrong to assert *no Indian agricultural commodity is ever regulated by a legal price floor*. The narrow finding concerns the requested across-the-board MSP guarantee.
- A comprehensive primary legal review was **not** completed of every central and state Act, rule and order in force on 1 July 2024. If an enacted general MSP-right statute covering the requested entitlement is produced, revisit the provisional verdict.
- The **26 July** government answer is post-claim context, not a document Gandhi possessed on 1 July.
