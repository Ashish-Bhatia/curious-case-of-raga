01Borders / By Rahul Gandhi2025-04-03
Unverified

China and the 4,000 sq km claim

The statement is documented. The precise area is not independently established by the sources reviewed.

THE CLAIM · PARAPHRASED

Rahul Gandhi said China was occupying 4,000 square kilometres of Indian territory.

Read the evidenceClose evidence↗

Context

Congress published his 3 April 2025 Lok Sabha intervention. It repeats the 4,000 sq km figure. A party transcript establishes what Congress says he said, not whether the territorial measurement is correct.

Evidence

In February 2025, the Defence Ministry rejected his attribution of territorial remarks to the Army Chief. It distinguished disrupted patrolling from the words Gandhi attributed to the chief. This is the government’s response, not an independent boundary survey.

Assessment

Keep two questions separate: whether the Army Chief made the attributed statement, and whether China occupies the claimed area. These sources do not independently verify the precise 4,000 sq km figure. A government denial alone does not disprove it.

What remains unverified

No independent geospatial calculation or agreed baseline was obtained. This assessment concerns the April 2025 claim, not a current map of territorial control.

Sources

  • Congress, statement of 3 April 2025 ↗
  • Defence Ministry, response of 4 February 2025 ↗

Read the evidenceClose evidence↗

Context

The source speech was delivered in Kanpur on 10 May 2024. AFP traced the circulating clip to a Congress livestream and compared the relevant passages.

Evidence

03Jobs / By Rahul Gandhi2024-03-07
Unverified

Thirty lakh vacancies: what was being counted?

The pledge is documented. The reviewed sources do not supply an auditable total of 30 lakh distinct vacant posts.

THE CLAIM · PARAPHRASED

Rahul Gandhi said there were 30 lakh government vacancies and pledged to fill them if elected.

Read the evidenceClose evidence↗

Context

At Banswara on 7 March 2024, Gandhi said Congress had counted 30 lakh government vacancies, according to contemporaneous Indian Express reporting. The April manifesto repeated the figure and listed central government posts, educational and medical institutions, and the Central Armed Police Forces. This check tests the claimed vacancy count, not whether a future government would fulfil the pledge.

Evidence

Lok Sabha answer 1093, dated 26 July 2023, reports 9,64,359 vacant regular civilian Union government posts as of 1 March 2022. In a separate Indian Express interview, Congress manifesto committee member Praveen Chakravarty acknowledged about 9.6 lakh central vacancies and said additional sectors brought the total to 30 lakh. That interview explains the party’s intended scope but supplies no itemised calculation.

Different dates and coverage. These figures are not a like-for-like comparison.
RecordReference dateCountCoverage
Government reply1 March 20229,64,359Regular civilian Union employees
Campaign claim7 March 2024 statement; count date unspecifiedAbout 30,00,000Broader sectors described by Congress

Assessment

The official figure is older and has a defined employee category. It does not, by itself, refute the broader campaign total. Equally, listing additional sectors does not verify their numbers or establish that they do not overlap with the official count. The claim remains unverified on the evidence reviewed.

What remains unverified

Required to settle the claim: an institution-by-institution list, a common reference date, sanctioned and filled strengths, and removal of duplicate posts. Original rally footage was not independently played. No verdict on the promise’s feasibility or delivery is assigned.

False

What the Supreme Court did not say on Rafale

The court did not make the finding attributed to it.

THE CLAIM · PARAPHRASED

The Supreme Court had endorsed the accusation behind Rahul Gandhi’s “chowkidar chor hai” slogan.

Read the evidenceClose evidence↗

Context

The April 2019 decision concerned consideration of documents in the Rafale review proceedings. It did not endorse Gandhi’s accusation against Narendra Modi.

Evidence

Contemporaneous reporting of the November 2019 judgment records the court rejecting the attribution. It also records Gandhi’s unconditional apology and closure of the contempt proceedings with a caution.

06Economy / By Rahul Gandhi2017-09-08
Supported

Did roughly 99% of the banned cash return?

The rounded return figure is supported. It does not establish the source of the money or the intent behind demonetisation.

THE CLAIM · PARAPHRASED

Rahul Gandhi said RBI had reported that about 99% of the old currency had returned to banks.

Read the evidenceClose evidence↗

Context

ANI’s report of Gandhi’s 8 September 2017 Parbhani speech attributes the 99% figure to him. The statement concerned the old ₹500 and ₹1,000 notes withdrawn in November 2016. His wider speech also alleged that demonetisation converted illicit money into legitimate money. That allegation is separate from the return percentage.

Evidence

RBI’s 2017–18 currency-management chapter, paragraph VIII.20, records ₹15,310.73 billion returned after processing. Lok Sabha answer 4023 of 4 January 2019 gives both the reconciled original value, ₹15,417.93 billion, and the returned value. Dividing the latter by the former gives 99.3047%, or 99.3% rounded to one decimal place. These later records corroborate the broad numerical claim, rather than reproduce the provisional figures available at the 2017 speech.

Assessment

Supported for the narrow statement that approximately 99% of the specified notes returned, measured by value. A high return rate does not show that every deposit was lawful, that every illicit holding was identified, or that demonetisation was designed to help particular people. Those conclusions require separate evidence.

What remains unverified

The statement’s attribution relies on ANI’s contemporaneous report, not independent playback of the complete speech. The calculation uses later reconciled figures. It is not a verdict on the overall economic costs, benefits or legality of demonetisation.

How we check

02 — THE METHOD

One claim at a time.

We test specific factual statements. Opinions, predictions and campaign promises need a different test. A verdict applies to the named claim, not to a person’s character.

Read beyond the clip

Find the original statement, its date and its context. Label paraphrases. State when original footage was not independently reviewed.

Show the evidence

Prefer original records. Identify party statements and government rebuttals as interested sources. Credit published fact checks when relying on their work.

Leave room for uncertainty

False means evidence contradicts the claim. Misleading means missing context changes its meaning. Unverified means the reviewed evidence is insufficient. Supported means the evidence backs the specific claim.

This is a small, selected collection, not a complete record or a measure of Rahul Gandhi’s overall accuracy. Historical checks do not imply coverage through today. This site has no affiliation with Rahul Gandhi, Congress or another political party.
