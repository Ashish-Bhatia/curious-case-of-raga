THE CLAIM · PARAPHRASED

Read the evidence & historyClose evidence & history↗

Context

Gandhi criticised the protection during the December 2025 electoral-reforms debate. This checks the existence of that provision, not the legality of his proposed retrospective response.

Historical position

Institutional history; incomplete all-law comparison

The pre-2014 institutional baseline was the 1991 Election Commission law, later amended to regulate a multi-member commission. Its subject matter was service conditions and the transaction of business. It was in force during UPA as well as the preceding NDA period.

This review has not reconstructed every pre-2014 statutory defence, procedural sanction or judicial immunity potentially available to commissioners. Absence of an identical provision in one statute would not establish absence of all legal protection.

What the comparison tells us

The valid comparison is the specific statutory wording and scope. Claims that commissioners previously had no protection, or that Congress could lawfully prosecute every official decision, require further legal analysis.

Assessment

Supported for the existence of official-duty protection. Describing it as unlimited immunity for everything a commissioner does would exceed the text.

Article 15(5): an existing power, not an automatic quota

The enabling provision exists. It has exceptions and requires legislation.

THE CLAIM · PARAPHRASED

Article 15(5) already provides a constitutional basis for reservation in private educational institutions.

Read the evidence & historyClose evidence & history↗

Context

Congress’s account of Gandhi’s 30 April 2025 briefing, published on 22 May, records him citing Article 15(5) and asking for implementation. This check concerns the existence and scope of the provision.

Historical position

Policy or statistical comparison

This enabling power dates to UPA-I. The 93rd Amendment, assented to on 20 January 2006, inserted Article 15(5), covering special admission provisions made by law, including for private aided and unaided institutions, with the stated minority-institution exception.

UPA also enacted the Central Educational Institutions (Reservation in Admission) Act, 2006, notified in January 2007. Its defined central-institution scope must not be confused with all private colleges. Section 3 sets SC, ST and OBC percentages, subject to exclusions and implementation provisions.

What the comparison tells us

Gandhi is invoking an existing constitutional power established under Congress-led government. Whether Congress or later governments fully used that power in each category of private institution is a separate implementation question.

Historical sources

  • Gazette, 93rd Amendment, 20 January 2006, section 2 ↗
  • India Code, Central Educational Institutions Act, 2006, sections 3–6 ↗

Evidence for this claim

The official Gazette’s 93rd Amendment text inserts Article 15(5). It permits special admission provisions made by law for socially and educationally backward classes, Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, including in private aided or unaided institutions. It excludes minority institutions protected by Article 30(1). The constitutional power is enabling, rather than an automatic uniform quota.

Assessment

Supported on the narrow claim that the constitutional power already exists. The provision does not itself impose one uniform quota in every private institution. The implementing law and the institution’s status matter.

What remains unverified

This does not establish which institutions were complying in April 2025, what further legislation was required in each jurisdiction, or whether a particular proposed quota would survive legal challenge.
