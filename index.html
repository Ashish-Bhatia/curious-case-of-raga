Does the 2023 law protect election commissioners?

Section 16 provides a substantial protection, with an official-duty connection in its wording.

THE CLAIM · PARAPHRASED

The 2023 law gives election commissioners protection against legal proceedings for official-duty acts.

Read the evidenceClose evidence↗

Context

Gandhi criticised the protection during the December 2025 electoral-reforms debate. This checks the existence of that provision, not the legality of his proposed retrospective response.

Evidence

Section 16 bars courts from entertaining or continuing civil or criminal proceedings against current or former CECs and ECs for acts, things or words while acting, or purporting to act, in official duty or function. The parliamentary bill text inspected here and Indian Express’s quotation of the enacted section agree. The wording contains no express good-faith qualifier.

Assessment

Supported for the existence of official-duty protection. Describing it as unlimited immunity for everything a commissioner does would exceed the text.

What remains unverified

This does not establish misconduct, rule out every form of judicial review, or determine how a court would apply the provision to particular facts. The enacted India Code PDF was not retrievable; no later court outcome is certified.

Article 15(5): an existing power, not an automatic quota

The enabling provision exists. It has exceptions and requires legislation.

THE CLAIM · PARAPHRASED

Article 15(5) already provides a constitutional basis for reservation in private educational institutions.

Read the evidenceClose evidence↗

Context

Congress’s account of Gandhi’s 30 April 2025 briefing, published on 22 May, records him citing Article 15(5) and asking for implementation. This check concerns the existence and scope of the provision.

Evidence

A January 2006 government explanation of the 93rd Amendment states that legislation can make special admission provisions for socially and educationally backward classes, Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, including in aided or unaided private institutions. What remains unverified

This does not establish which institutions were complying in April 2025, what further legislation was required in each jurisdiction, or whether a particular proposed quota would survive legal challenge.

14Economy / By Rahul Gandhi2024-04-24
Misleading

Loan write-offs are not the same as loan waivers

An aggregate bank write-off figure does not establish that borrowers’ debts were cancelled or identify who benefited.

THE CLAIM · PARAPHRASED

Rahul Gandhi said Modi had waived ₹16 lakh crore of loans for his billionaire friends.

Read the evidenceClose evidence↗

Context

PTI reported Gandhi’s 24 April 2024 Hindi post as alleging ₹16 lakh crore in loan waivers for billionaire friends. The check concerns the distinction between write-offs and waivers, and what a banking total establishes. It does not certify an alternative total or a list of recipients.

Evidence

RBI’s 8 June 2023 framework defines a technical write-off as an accounting action that leaves the claim against the borrower and the right to recover intact. It separately defines a compromise settlement, which involves an agreed settlement and may involve giving up part of a claim. The regulator’s distinction was already in force when Gandhi made the statement.

Different actions, different effects. An account-level record is needed to establish which occurred.
ActionEffect on the debt
Technical write-offRemoved from the lender’s accounts for accounting purposes; borrower remains liable.
Compromise settlementNegotiated settlement; lender may give up part of its claim.
WaiverLender gives up the relevant repayment claim. A write-off total alone does not establish this.

Assessment

Treating bank write-offs as proof of government loan forgiveness is misleading. Write-off data alone cannot establish ₹16 lakh crore of cancelled liabilities, a government order cancelling them, or that all recipients were billionaires. This does not show that every written-off loan was recovered or that banks suffered no losses. Low recoveries and the fairness of settlements remain valid questions requiring their own evidence.

What remains unverified

The precise ₹16 lakh crore total, its period, the mix of write-offs and settlements, and the alleged beneficiaries were not independently reconstructed in this check. Some settlements do reduce a borrower’s liability. Do not assume every accounting write-off is a technical write-off without inspecting the underlying account. Attribution relies on PTI’s report; the original X post was not independently retrieved.

Sources

  • PTI via Business Standard, Gandhi’s post, 24 April 2024 ↗
  • RBI, Framework for Compromise Settlements and Technical Write-offs, 8 June 2023, Annex paragraph 1 ↗

How we check

02 — THE METHOD

One claim at a time.

We test specific factual statements. Opinions, predictions and campaign promises need a different test. A verdict applies to the named claim, not to a person’s character.

Read beyond the clip

Find the original statement, its date and its context. Label paraphrases. State when original footage was not independently reviewed.

Show the evidence

Prefer original records. Identify party statements and government rebuttals as interested sources. Credit published fact checks when relying on their work.

Leave room for uncertainty

False means evidence contradicts the claim. Misleading means missing context changes its meaning. Unverified means the reviewed evidence is insufficient. Supported means the evidence backs the specific claim.
